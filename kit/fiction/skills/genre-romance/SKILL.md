# Romance

Layers on top of the audience profile. Where this skill and the audience profile disagree on length or content limits, the audience profile wins. Romance is defined by a central love story and an ending that pays it off, and editors stress that there is no formula.

## Reader promise
- ROM-PROM-01: Every romance has two basic elements, a central love story and an emotionally satisfying and optimistic ending. [S1][S2]
- ROM-PROM-02: The love story must be the main focus. A romantic thread that is not central makes the book something other than genre romance, and a tragic ending disqualifies it. [S2]
- ROM-PROM-03: Plan a clear happily-ever-after (HEA). Social media discussion suggests readers hold to a conventional HEA more firmly than the official wording requires. A softer happy-for-now (HFN) ending is allowed only if the Ending section of `bible/world/brief.md` says so (house setting; no source read defines HFN). [S1]
- ROM-PROM-04: The difference between a romance and a romantic subplot is the ending: the love story is resolved in a happy ending, an HEA or, where ROM-PROM-03 allows it, an HFN. A story that leaves the couple apart or in doubt is not a romance. [S5]
- ROM-PROM-05: The couple's emotional issues should be the driving force of the tension between them, and the books are meant to be emotionally intense. [S3]

## Structure
- ROM-STR-01: Regis's eight elements are society defined, meeting, barrier, attraction, declaration, point of ritual death, recognition and betrothal. They can appear in any order and also work for a romantic subplot. [S5]
- ROM-STR-02: The barrier is what keeps the couple apart. Ideally it is both internal and external and shown across several scenes, and it creates the narrative tension that keeps readers moving forward. [S5][S6]
- ROM-STR-03: The point of ritual death, known to authors as the dark moment, is a real or symbolic loss that makes the happy ending seem impossible. [S5][S6]
- ROM-STR-04: Recognition is a realisation that leads a character to prove their commitment, and it often triggers the grand gesture. The betrothal must make the commitment clear, for example through a kiss, a proposal or a date invitation. [S5]
- ROM-STR-05: Hayes's Romancing the Beat has four phases: setup, falling in love, retreating from love and fighting for love. The beats include meet cute, no way, adhesion, midpoint of love, break up, dark night of the soul, grand gesture and whole-hearted ending. [S4]
- ROM-STR-06: Treat beat sheets as a recipe, not a rule. The editors say there is no formula for a publishable romance, and the shared patterns are elements, not events. [S7][S2]
- ROM-STR-07: The meeting usually comes early, and characters who already know each other still need an on-page meeting. Put the first on-page meeting within the first 20% of the manuscript (house setting). [S5]

## Conventions
- ROM-CONV-01: Show why each lead resists love. In Hayes's structure the no way beats set up the reasons to avoid love, adhesion binds the couple, and the fears come true at the shields up beat. [S4]
- ROM-CONV-02: Keep conflicts believable and relatable, and fitted to the period or setting. [S3]
- ROM-CONV-03: Secondary characters enrich the story but must not pull attention from the couple. [S3]
- ROM-CONV-04: Chemistry and sexual tension are required at every sensuality level, from sweet to spicy, and are always subject to the audience profile's content limits. [S3]
- ROM-CONV-05: The declaration scenes have each character confess their feelings, together or separately, in a way that suits who they are. [S5]
- ROM-CONV-06: Subgenre labels such as historical, contemporary and paranormal describe the setting, not the events. A meet-cute, a first kiss and a rival love interest are not universal, so do not require them. [S2]
- ROM-CONV-07: RWA's seven subgenres are contemporary, erotic, historical, inspirational, paranormal, romantic suspense and young adult, and category romance is a shorter form issued in numbered series. Confirm the target line before setting length. [S1]
- ROM-CONV-08: One category line, Mills & Boon Historical, asks for 70,000 to 75,000 words. The sources read here give no figure for other lines, so use the target line's own guidance for them. [S3]

## Pitfalls
- ROM-PIT-01: A romance added as an afterthought, a check-the-box romance, leaves readers disappointed and rewriting the story in their heads. Reviewers may react the same way. [S5]
- ROM-PIT-02: Skipping the essential beats makes the romantic thread read as weak, because romance readers have clear expectations. [S5]
- ROM-PIT-03: A love story with no barrier can be sweet and wonderful and still not be a good romance narrative. [S6]
- ROM-PIT-04: Heavy historical or setting research must not crowd out the romance. Editors say to remember to focus on the romance. [S3]
- ROM-PIT-05: Ending with the couple's future in doubt, or tragically, breaks the genre promise. [S2][S1]
- ROM-PIT-06: Secondary characters who take over from the couple weaken the central love story. [S3]

## Reader psychology
Builds on the reader-engagement skill.
- ROM-ENG-01: By the end of the setup (first 25% of the manuscript, house setting) both leads are on the page and the text shows or states why at least one of them resists love. [S4]
  Check: find the line where a lead says or shows why they will not fall in love and confirm it falls before the 25% mark.
- ROM-ENG-02: Every scene shared by the leads moves them closer or pushes them apart. The push and pull of attraction against the barrier drives the story. [S5]
  Check: for each shared scene, write one phrase naming the pull (attraction) or the push (barrier).
- ROM-ENG-03: The barrier is visible across the middle of the book, at least once in each quarter of the manuscript (house setting). [S5][S6]
  Check: list a scene in each quarter where the barrier appears; the check fails if any quarter has none.
- ROM-ENG-04: Before the final act there is a point of ritual death, a moment when the couple's happy ending looks impossible to the reader. [S5]
  Check: point to the scene and sentence where a lead or the reader can reasonably believe the couple is finished.
- ROM-ENG-05: The ending shows unmistakable commitment on the page, such as a kiss, a proposal or a date invitation. [S5]
  Check: the final chapter contains an on-page act or statement of commitment by both leads, and no line leaves their future in doubt.
