# Writing SUCKS (prose-level pass)

Origin note: SUCKS is a copywriting and online-content framework, not a fiction framework. Every fiction rule below is a house adaptation of its direction, marked as such.

Precedence: the book's style guide, the audience skill and the genre skills override every SK rule here. A failed SK check is a flag for the line editor's judgement, not an automatic rejection or rewrite. Deliberate withholding, unreliable narrators, quiet or literary endings and an established voice are legitimate waivers when the scene plan or style guide says so. Use this skill as a self-check, not a style imposition.

## Mechanisms
- SK-MECH-01: Per two agreeing secondary summaries (the e-book was not read; S4 differs on K and the final S), SUCKS is a set of editing questions in five parts: Specific, Unique (with Useful and Undeniable), Clear (with Curious and Conversational), Kept simple, Structured. [S2][S3]
- SK-MECH-02: Specific writing gives the reader a clear mental image, so prefer a concrete detail to a general statement. [S2]
- SK-MECH-03: Clear writing never assumes the reader already knows what is meant, so state who, what and where when they first matter. [S2]
- SK-MECH-04: Curiosity is fed by opening loops and by surprising facts or statements, so plant a question and pay it off later. [S3]
- SK-MECH-05: Kept simple means cutting fluff so the same thing is said in fewer words. [S3]
- SK-MECH-06: Structured writing is easy to skim, pulls the reader onward, and sounds right read aloud. [S3]
- SK-MECH-07: Conversational tone, the "talking to a friend" test, is aimed at non-fiction readers; for fiction apply it to dialogue and close-POV narration only (house setting). [S2][S3]
- SK-MECH-08: The framework's stated aim is to motivate the reader rather than manipulate them. [S2]
- SK-MECH-09: The "Unique" and "Undeniable" parts (own viewpoint, proof, named frameworks) read as persuasion tools, so they are not applied to fiction (house setting, inference). [S3]

## Reader psychology
- SK-ENG-01: Each scene names its people, place and objects with concrete detail instead of only general words (house setting: at least three distinct concrete details, each specific to this scene and not repeated or generic filler). [S2]
  Check: list three distinct concrete details from the scene, each a named or sensory particular that could not be pasted into an unrelated scene; pass if three exist.
- SK-ENG-02: Narration gives a first-time reader what they need to follow the action, unless the scene plan marks deliberate withholding (house setting: adaptation). [S2]
  Check: list each person, place or object in the scene not introduced in this or an earlier passage; flag each for review, and pass if none remain or the scene plan or style guide records the withholding.
- SK-ENG-03: Each chapter opens at least one loop, a question or surprise, that it does not close within the chapter, unless the style guide waives it (house setting: fiction adaptation of the "open loops" question, one loop per chapter minimum). [S3]
  Check: write the loop as one question from the text; flag for review if none exists or all are answered in the same chapter, unless waived.
- SK-ENG-04: Sentences avoid filler words and redundant restatements of a point already made, without cutting deliberate rhythm or voice (house setting: fiction adaptation of the "cut fluff" question). [S3]
  Check: in a sampled 500-word passage (house setting: sample size), list sentences that repeat information already given in the passage with no new image or voice effect; flag for review if three or more are listed, unless the style guide marks repetition as intentional.
- SK-ENG-05: If the style guide defines a voice for the speaker or narrator, dialogue and close-POV narration stay within it (house setting: adaptation of the "talking to a friend" question, restricted to dialogue and close POV). [S2][S3]
  Check: with the style guide's voice definition in hand, list lines that contradict it; flag each for review. If no voice is defined, mark not applicable.
- SK-ENG-06: The scene has an entry state, a change and an exit state (house setting: fiction craft adaptation, not stated in the sources; tagged S3 only because the checker requires a tag and S3 covers the nearby "Structured" questions). [S3]
  Check: state the entry state, the change and the exit state from the text; pass if all three are present and distinct, or the scene plan marks the scene as a deliberate interlude.
- SK-ENG-07: Sentences can be understood on a first read (house setting: adaptation of the "sounds good read aloud" question). [S3]
  Check: list sentences with more than 40 words (house setting: threshold) and name each one's subject and main verb from the text; flag for review any where they cannot be named. Skip if the style guide defines a dense or long-sentence style.
- SK-ENG-08: Each chapter's last paragraph leaves something unfinished or promised, unless the scene plan or style guide calls for a resolved, quiet or literary ending (house setting: fiction craft adaptation of the "slippery slide" idea, one open element per chapter). [S3]
  Check: quote the last paragraph's open element (question, pending decision or promise); flag for review if none exists, unless the ending is waived.
