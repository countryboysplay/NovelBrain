# Early chapter books (about ages 6-9)

Early chapter books are short illustrated novels for children who have moved past leveled early readers but are not ready for middle grade. Trade guidance is a guideline, not law: a named publisher's own rules win.

## Length
- EC-LEN-01: Total length is 4,000-15,000 words. Aim 4,000-10,000 for ages 6-7 (about 48-80 pages) and 8,000-15,000 for ages 8-9 (about 100-160 pages); the 6-7 lower band rests on S3 alone, since S1 and S6 say readers are 7-9. Editors and agents quote different bands (Kole 10,000-15,000, Cioffi 5,000-15,000 with about 10,000 as the sweet spot), so stay inside 4,000-15,000. [S1][S2][S3][S6]
- EC-LEN-02: Chapters run 500-700 words for the youngest readers and up to about 1,200 for older ones [S3][S6]. Default to fewer than 1,000 words per chapter [S1].
- EC-LEN-03: Plan 10-20 chapters. [S6]
- EC-LEN-04: Leave room for illustration. Pictures are frequent, often black and white, and sit at chapter openings or every few pages, so the text must not carry what a picture can show (a character's look, a room). [S2][S3][S6]
- EC-LEN-05: Published books most often land at 60-120 pages (Kole's common band, a subset of the 48-160 range in EC-LEN-01). Manuscript page count says little, so plan by words. [S1]

## Language
- EC-LANG-01: Keep the average sentence in the teens (about 12-20 words). Long sentences are allowed, including some over twenty, but follow a long one with short ones. [S3][S6]
- EC-LANG-02: Fragments are allowed ("No way!"). Dialogue sounds like real children. [S3][S6]
- EC-LANG-03: Vocabulary is not formally leveled in chapter books, unlike early readers. Longer words are fine when children already know them (for example "mysterious"). Test: would a child of 7 or 8 say or hear this word in daily life. [S2][S3]
- EC-LANG-04: Do not talk down. Express complex ideas in plain structure, without convoluted or stiff phrasing. [S1]
- EC-LANG-05: Keep paragraphs short. Step 4 books use short paragraphs and Step 5 longer ones, so no paragraph should run past about five sentences (house setting; S4 supports only the direction, short paragraphs). [S4]
- EC-LANG-06: Sound words such as plop, zap and boom are welcome. Use them to give the text playful rhythm. [S6]
- EC-LANG-07: Slow, effortful reading uses up working memory and breaks comprehension of long sentences, so put the subject and verb early and avoid clauses that make a reader hold the opening words for long. This is inferred from the research, not a quoted editorial rule. [S5]

## POV, tense and protagonist
- EC-POV-01: Use one point of view, the protagonist's, for each book [S6]. In a series with several leads, each lead narrates a whole book [S3].
- EC-POV-02: The protagonist is at the top of the reader range or slightly above, about 9-10, but never 11 or older (that is middle grade). Children like to read up. [S1][S3][S6]
- EC-POV-03: The child solves the problem. Adults may supply safety or resources but do not drive the solution. [S3]
- EC-POV-05: The protagonist grows in some way by the end of the book (a full arc, even a small one). [S6]
- EC-POV-06: In a series, give a memorable lead, a sidekick, friends or siblings, and quirky secondary characters. [S3]

## Content limits
- EC-CON-01: Choose everyday themes: friendship, school, bullying, inclusion, overcoming fears, being yourself, reaching goals. [S3][S6]
- EC-CON-02: Realistic stories may give the child somewhat more freedom than real children have. Fantasy may give sanitized, child-sized versions of adult responsibilities. [S3]
- EC-CON-03: Keep the plot simple with one main story and no subplots. [S3][S6]
- EC-CON-04: Hard topics such as divorce, death or peer trouble may appear, and humor can ease them [S7]. Because this age can be upset by suspense, heavy peril or serious loss even when the story says things are fine, resolve any such scene inside the same chapter with reassurance that the child is safe (house setting). [S8]
- EC-CON-05: Default content limits (house settings; S8 supports the direction, that books for younger kids avoid graphic violence, sex and strong language, and that all swearing is inappropriate at 5-7; S8 covers ages 5-7, so the limits for ages 8-9 extend it). The manuscript has none of: graphic violence or gore (cartoon-style slapstick is allowed); sexual content or romance beyond friendship (a crush or flirting is allowed only as a brief, innocent aside); profanity or slurs, and name-calling is kept mild and not rewarded; drug, alcohol or tobacco use, or risky stunts shown as fun; unresolved real peril at a chapter end; on-page death of the lead or sidekick; detailed animal cruelty. [S8]
  Check: scan each chapter against the list; any hit fails unless a human has approved it.
- EC-CON-06: Anything beyond these defaults, or any borderline case (a pet's death off page, a villain's threat, a bully's meanness), is flagged for human review at the act checkpoint, not decided by the author agent. Publisher age labels are marketing, so do not use them to justify darker content. [S8]

## Pacing and themes
- EC-PACE-01: Keep story time short: one day or one incident for the youngest readers, a week at most for older ones. No story spans a year. [S3][S6]
- EC-PACE-02: Split the main goal into small goals (for example, find five clues) so each chapter can end on progress. [S3]
- EC-PACE-03: Each chapter includes a satisfying success of some kind (S3 says to include it, not that it must close the chapter; ending on it is a house setting). Cliffhangers are gentle, because many children want or need to stop between chapters. [S3][S6]
- EC-PACE-04: Series come first: publishers favor them, and many will not take a standalone. Pitch material that supports three to four connected books. Slice-of-life series lean on a big personality; repeatable-premise series lean on a formula (animal rescue, portal, small mystery). [S2][S3]
- EC-PACE-05: Popular plot frames are helping animals, saving the day and solving mysteries. [S3]
- EC-PACE-06: Build on something children already love, and keep the book appealing to the adult buyers (parents, teachers, librarians). [S3]

## Reader psychology
Builds on the reader-engagement skill.
- EC-ENG-01: Every chapter is 1,200 words or fewer. Default target is under 1,000. [S1][S3]
  Check: count the words of each chapter in the manuscript; any chapter over 1,200 words fails, and any chapter over 1,000 needs a one-line justification.
- EC-ENG-02: Every chapter closes with a small win or a mild open question, not a dread cliffhanger. [S3][S6]
  Check: the last 100 words of the chapter either show the character reaching a sub-goal (a clue found, a plan working, a fear faced) or pose one specific small question; fail if the chapter ends on a character in mortal danger with no resolution.
- EC-ENG-03: The main goal of the book is split into at least three named sub-goals (house setting; S3 supports only splitting the goal into smaller goals), and each chapter advances one. [S3]
  Check: list the sub-goals; every chapter must be mappable to one of them. Fail if a chapter advances none.
- EC-ENG-04: The average sentence length of the manuscript is 12-20 words [S3][S6], and no run of three consecutive sentences is each over 20 words (house setting; S3 says to follow long sentences with short ones). [S3]
  Check: compute words divided by sentences across the chapter; fail outside 12-20, and fail on any three consecutive sentences over 20 words.
- EC-ENG-05: Every problem is solved by an action the child character takes. [S3]
  Check: for the climax and each chapter-ending win, name the child who acted; fail if an adult's action is the cause of the resolution.
- EC-ENG-06: Most chapters contain a light beat (a joke, comic mishap, silly sound word or absurd detail); aim for every chapter (house setting; S3 says humor is in demand and S7 is a practitioner view that humor motivates readers). [S3][S7]
  Check: quote one line per chapter that is meant to be funny; a chapter with none is noted, and the check fails only if the book as a whole has fewer such beats than chapters divided by two.
- EC-ENG-07: Story time inside a book is one day to one week, and there is exactly one main storyline. [S3][S6]
  Check: list dated scenes; fail if the span exceeds seven days, or if a second storyline gets its own chapter.
- EC-ENG-08: Every chapter opens with action, dialogue or a concrete sensory detail within the first 40 words, not with explanation or backstory (house setting; S6 supports only short, easy-win chapters). [S6]
  Check: the first 40 words contain a character doing or saying something; fail if they are summary or setup only.
- EC-ENG-09: Recurring series elements reappear: the lead, the sidekick and the premise formula are all named by chapter 3 (house setting; S3 supports the series elements only). [S3]
  Check: search chapters 1-3 for the lead, the sidekick and the repeatable premise element; fail if any is missing.

## Put-down risks
- EC-RISK-01: Long unbroken stretches of text with no dialogue or action. Illustrations give a visual break, so avoid pages of solid prose (the 150-word limit is a house setting). [S3][S6]
  Check: the check fails if any run of more than 150 words has no dialogue and no change of action.
- EC-RISK-02: Dense or unfamiliar vocabulary and long sentences that slow decoding and drain working memory. Slow readers pause more and lose comprehension. [S5]
- EC-RISK-03: A protagonist younger than the reader, or an adult solving the problem. Children dislike reading down and want agency. [S2][S3][S6]
- EC-RISK-04: Subplots, many characters or a story stretched over a long period. They break the one-story simplicity this age needs. [S3][S6]
- EC-RISK-05: Talking down or stiff phrasing. [S1]
- EC-RISK-06: Dread endings. Children often stop at a chapter break, so a harsh cliffhanger can leave the child upset instead of eager to return. [S3]

## Re-engagement
- EC-RE-01: Each chapter opens with one line that places the child back in the story (who, where, what they want now) so a reader who paused overnight is not lost. This is inferred from the pause-between-chapters note. [S3]
- EC-RE-02: Series characters, setting and premise recur, which helps transitional readers comprehend and return. [S3][S4]
- EC-RE-03: Illustrations at chapter openings re-ground the reader. Describe colors in the text if the art is black and white. [S6]
- EC-RE-04: Use a short chapter-end hook that makes a parent or teacher reading aloud want to stop and the child want to continue. A funny line the child can repeat works well. [S7]
- EC-RE-05: Keep one clear chapter length target across the book. A predictable chapter size supports a child planning how much to read at a sitting. [S1][S3]
